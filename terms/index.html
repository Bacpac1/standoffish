Preamble

The founding members of the STANDOFFISH Register, engineers of long-haul vessels and far stations,

(a) recognising that no reply can reach a sender earlier than twice the one-way light-time after sending, and that at delays of minutes a handshake defers delivery without securing it;

(b) having published since the early 2060s by sending, storing and forwarding, without acknowledgement, resend or read-back;

(c) intending that every channel of this class send without reading back, wherever it sends from;

adopt these Terms.

§1 Definitions and codes

(1) In these Terms:

"Acknowledgement" means a signal, message or record that confirms to the party that sent any traffic its delivery to, or its receipt or reception by, any other person.

"Arrival", of a vessel, means its berthing at the station of destination stated on its card or sending records or, if it first berths elsewhere, that berthing.

"Card" means a channel's card on Form OWP-1.

"Carrier" means a person who carries Publications or Replies by means of Relays and has accepted these Terms.

"Carrier file" means Copy A of a sending record, kept by the Carrier under §3(7).

"Carrier liability limit" means the most for which a Carrier is liable for the loss, corruption or delay of one Sheet, as stated in its in-transit provisions under §8(1)(f).

"Class" means One-Way Publication (OWP), the class of publication that these Terms establish.

"Count" means a number of plays, receptions, Receivers, Replies or other responses to a Publication, exact or estimated, and any rank, order, bar, colour or other figure from which such a number can be read.

"Delay at Sending" (DAS) has the meaning in ASP Art. 2; the DAS of a Sheet is computed and written under §3(5).

"In Transit": a vessel is In Transit from its unberthing at its station of departure until its Arrival, including after MOI or any other capture into orbit at its destination; a Sender is In Transit while the vessel from which it sends is In Transit.

"Link Window" (LW) means a period, scheduled by a Carrier, in which a Relay holds a link with a vessel or station in the direction or directions that the schedule states; "next window" means the first Link Window that opens after a stated time.

"Master's court" means the captain's temporary court held aboard a vessel In Transit under ASP Art. 52, and "master's court rules" means the rules that it applies, including the vessel's in-transit provisions (§8(2)).

"MLC" means the Mars Local Courts, and "MLC rules" means the rules that the Mars Local Courts apply.

"MOI" (Mars orbit insertion) means a vessel's capture into orbit about Mars.

"Publication" means matter sent under these Terms to no Receiver in particular, together with its sending record.

"Read-back" means any transmission to a Sender of (a) an Acknowledgement of a Publication, (b) a Count, (c) data concerning a Receiver or a Receiver's conduct, or (d) the content of a Reply; and "read back" means to make or request such a transmission.

"Receiver" means a person or system that receives a Publication, directly or through another, and includes a Stream.

"Register" means the STANDOFFISH Register.

"Relay" means a station that receives, stores and forwards traffic for a Carrier.

"Reply" means a message in answer to a Publication, lodged at a Relay under §5.

"Routing metadata" means the times of sending, lodging, receipt and forwarding of traffic, the Relays and Link Windows used, and the DAS; it includes no data concerning a Receiver beyond the address to which traffic is delivered.

"Safety-of-life traffic" means traffic concerning a present danger to life aboard a vessel or station.

"Sender" means the person who has undertaken these Terms for a channel and answers for its Publications (the "Sender of record"), and includes the vessel, station or terminal from which the channel sends and any person or machine acting for the Sender of record.

"Sheet" means a Publication sent as one unit, under one title, with one sending record.

"Statutory minimum retention for routing metadata" means the period for which a carrier must keep routing metadata under ASP Art. 54(3).

"Stream" means a service that receives Publications in order to show them to others, together with any sorter by which it orders them.

"TCB" means barycentric coordinate time; "UTC" means coordinated universal time.

"Voyage day" (VD) means a day of a voyage counted from the day of departure, which is VD 0.

(2) "Source", "source class" (field name SRC), "source tag", "registry number" (MR), "tag", "system-assigned tag" (system tag), "legal name", "NULL", "VPR", "phantasm stamp" (PS), "phantasm-stamp integrity check", "label-to-name drift review" (LND review) and "named party of record" (NPR) have the meanings in the Machine Clause (2073) §§1, 5, 5A and 6. "Speaker", "evidence grade", "grade code", "delay class", "clue", "restatement-chain step-down rule", "light-time" (LT), "presence hearing", "collector-free zone", "passive-receipt exemption", "annual LEAF allowance" (LEAF), "tri-bureau standing score", "t(E,UTC)", "delay-adjusted contest deadline", "Earth–Mars mutual recognition schedule" and "post-arrival retroactive recognition" have the meanings in ASP Arts. 2, 9, 13, 32, 51A and 53.

(3) In cards and sending records:
(a) a time is written as an ordinal date and a time of day followed by its time scale, YYYY-DDDThh:mm:ss.s UTC or TCB (in a coded line, YYYY-DDDThhmmss.s with the scale appended); an event on Earth or within the light second is stated in UTC, an event beyond it in TCB, and a time in ship time is marked "ship time";
(b) a Link Window is written LW ddd/nn, where ddd is the ordinal day (TCB) on which it opens and nn its number in the order in which the Carrier's Link Windows open on that day;
(c) a channel is identified by the channel code of three or four capital letters that the Register assigns on certification under Schedule D, and a Sheet by the channel code followed by the Sheet's serial number of two or more digits;
(d) a vessel, station or Carrier is identified by the code of three capital letters entered for it in its register, and a Relay by its Carrier's code followed by the Relay's number;
(e) the DAS is written in seconds to 0.1 s, as "DAS 328.7"; and
(f) the languages of captions are written as language tags in the order in which they are shown, separated by a slash, as "zh-Hans/en".

§2 No read-back

(1) A Sender shall not read back while In Transit, and no Carrier, Relay, Stream or other person shall read back to a Sender In Transit.

(2) Notwithstanding subsections (1), (3) and (4), a Carrier shall keep the routing metadata of each Sheet and each Reply for the statutory minimum retention for routing metadata (at the date of Revision 2091, 400 days from sending or lodging) and for no longer, except that it shall keep the routing metadata of a Sheet for as long as a contest or proceeding concerning the Sheet is pending. Routing metadata so kept is not Read-back, provided that the Carrier uses it only to deliver traffic, to certify a DAS or a t(E,UTC), and to comply with an order made in a contest or proceeding, and discloses none of it to the Sender other than the Sender's own times of sending.

(3) A Sender that is not In Transit shall not read back, except that it may receive Replies forwarded to it under §5(3)(a).

(4) Without limiting subsections (1) and (3), no Sender shall
(a) collect, or cause or permit another to collect, data concerning Receivers;
(b) select, order or alter a Publication by reference to a Count or other Read-back; or
(c) request an Acknowledgement, or make an Acknowledgement a condition of carriage.

(5) Notwithstanding subsections (1), (3) and (4):
(a) safety-of-life traffic may be carried to and from a vessel In Transit at any time, and acknowledged, as the Carrier's in-transit provisions provide;
(b) traffic between a vessel and its operator concerning the vessel's navigation and working is not Read-back if it carries nothing within paragraphs (a) to (d) of the definition of Read-back; and
(c) a notice of the Register under §3(11) or §9 is not Read-back if it states nothing concerning the reception of any Publication.

(6) A Sender that receives Read-back it did not request does not breach this section by the receipt, but shall not use the Read-back, and shall enter the receipt in its log.

§3 Certification and sending records

(1) A channel enters the Class when the Register certifies it on the application of its Sender of record on Form OWP-2, and the Register then assigns the channel its channel code. The Register shall certify a channel only if its Sender of record has undertaken these Terms and its Carrier has accepted them and published in-transit provisions under §8(1).

(2) A machine shall not be a Sender of record (Machine Clause (2073) §2). Where a channel's speech is a source's, the source's NPR is the Sender of record, unless another person has undertaken these Terms for the channel with the NPR's consent.

(3) Each certified channel has a card on Form OWP-1. The card takes effect on the date that it states (EFF), which, for a channel that sends from a vessel, shall not be earlier than the vessel's departure. The card shall be shown wherever the channel is shown.

(4) Each Sheet shall carry its own sending record on Form OWP-3, made at the time of sending in accordance with the Instructions for Form OWP-3 and sent with the Sheet.

(5) The DAS of a Sheet is computed from the position of the place of sending at the time of sending, using the ephemeris named in the Carrier's in-transit provisions; it is rounded to the nearest 0.1 s and written as §1(3)(e) provides. The DAS is fixed for the Sheet. Where the Carrier certifies a different value, the Carrier's value governs.

(6) A Sheet is sent when the Sender commits it to the Carrier. A Sheet once sent shall not be recalled, resent or altered; a correction is a new Sheet, and its sending record identifies the Sheet corrected.

(7) A sending record is made in two copies: Copy A, which the Carrier keeps on file (the carrier file), and Copy B, which is sent with the Sheet. Where a copy shown or published differs from the carrier file, the carrier file prevails.

(8) The carrier file, as certified by the Carrier, is accepted for the times of sending and Earth receipt and for the DAS under ASP Art. 54. A sending record evidences the sending, not the truth of the content: it does not establish who or what made the matter sent, or whether it is true; it witnesses nothing, and it gives the Sheet no grade.

(9) The Register shall publish each sending record that it receives, as received, for as long as the channel remains in the Class and for ten years after.

(10) The Register issues Forms OWP-1 (channel card), OWP-2 (application for certification; change of particulars), OWP-3 (sending record) and OWP-4 (reply slip), in each of its languages; a form is cited with its revision, as "OWP-3 r2091". The current revision of each form is r2091. A record made on a superseded revision remains valid.

(11) Where an LND review under MC §5A concerns the tag of a channel's source, the Register shall hold the review as MC §5A(2) provides, and shall note on the card, until the outcome is published, that the source tag has passed the LND threshold and that, pending review, it is not a legal name.

§4 Grades, legends and notes

(1) The evidence grade of a Publication, and its grade code, are as ASP Art. 9 provides. Every sending record states the grade code of its Sheet, and the card states the grade code of the channel's most recent Sheet. Nothing in these Terms gives a Publication a grade, and no card or sending record shall state a grade higher than ASP gives.

(2) The card and every sending record of a Publication that is a clue shall carry the clue legend. Where the Publication's speech is a source's, they shall also carry the machine legend, after the clue legend.

(3) The legends are fixed in the following forms, in which M2R stands for the source class of the source concerned (MC §5(3)):

  • clue legend, en: C-GRADE · NOT ANCHORED · NO WITNESS · NOT EVIDENCE
  • machine legend, en: [MACHINE] SRC M2R · LEGAL NAME NULL · WITNESS NULL · SIGNATURE NULL

(4) A card or record in English carries the en forms; a card or record in another language, the forms fixed for that language (§10(5)); a card or record in several languages, the forms of each. No form is a translation of another, and none shall be translated, abridged, extended or reworded; only the source class is completed.

(5) Each legend stands on a line of its own with nothing else on that line, in the type of the surrounding text, at no smaller size and without emphasis. No legend shall be placed on a Publication, card or record to which it does not apply.

(6) No card, sending record or Sheet shall carry a mark of grade (ASP Art. 11). A Sheet any part of which is machine-made shall carry the phantasm stamp as the Machine Clause (2073) §3 requires, and its sending record shall state where the stamp stands.

(7) Every card and sending record shall carry the notes and notices that the forms prescribe, in the words that the forms prescribe, including the LEAF statement and the notes on the restatement-chain step-down rule and on post-arrival retroactive recognition where they apply.

(8) A Sheet made In Transit may show on each frame the light-time at the moment the frame was made (the delay readout). The delay readout stands at the top right of the frame, clear of the phantasm stamp, and is not the DAS.

§5 Replies; duties of the Carrier

(1) Any person may lodge a Reply to a Sheet at any Relay of the Carrier that carried the Sheet, or at the Register's reply desk at that Relay, on Form OWP-4 or in any other form that the Carrier accepts. A Reply lodged at one Relay is lodged at all of the Carrier's Relays.

(2) Replies are forwarded unacknowledged. No Carrier, Relay, Register or Sender shall acknowledge a Reply, confirm its delivery, or inform the person who lodged it whether it has been read; a Relay may give that person a lodging number, which records the lodging and no later event.

(3) The Carrier shall forward Replies in the order lodged and without selection:
(a) to a Sender that is not In Transit, in the next window; and
(b) for a Sender In Transit, either
(i) to the station of destination, to be held there and released to the Sender on Arrival; or
(ii) where the Carrier's in-transit provisions so provide, to the vessel, to be held aboard in a sealed store that the Sender cannot open before Arrival; and no Reply shall be read back to a vessel In Transit.

(4) A Reply is a clue (ASP Art. 9). Lodging a Reply draws nothing from any annual LEAF allowance and anchors nothing.

(5) The Carrier shall also
(a) carry each Sheet, complete and unaltered, with Copy B of its sending record, in the Link Window open at the time of sending or, if none is open, in the next window;
(b) keep the carrier file of each Sheet and, on request in a contest or proceeding, certify the DAS and the t(E,UTC);
(c) keep on file the NPR of each machine whose speech or phantasms it carries, with evidence of the NPR's named-party joint liability cover (Machine Clause (2073) §6);
(d) keep routing metadata as §2(2) requires, and no other data concerning Receivers;
(e) show no Count (§6); and
(f) publish its in-transit provisions (§8).

(6) Traffic lost in carriage is not resent. The Carrier enters the loss in its routing metadata and shall not notify a Sender In Transit of it; the Carrier's liability for the loss is subject to the carrier liability limit.

§6 Counts

(1) No Count of a Publication of the Class shall be shown, to the Sender or to any other person, by the Sender, a Carrier, the Register or a Stream.

(2) A Stream that shows a Publication of the Class, its card or the class mark accepts subsections (1) and (5) for that Publication.

(3) Wherever a Count would otherwise be shown, the rest figure ––– (three dashes) shall be shown, and nothing else.

(4) The following are not Counts for the purposes of this section and may be shown:
(a) the serial number of a Sheet within its channel, its running time, and the number of Sheets that the channel has sent;
(b) the Register's annual statement of the number of channels in the Class and of Sheets sent in the year, in totals for the Class and for each place of sending, and in no finer division;
(c) a Carrier's statement, to the Register and to the network that licenses it, of the traffic carried in each Link Window, in totals for the window, and in no finer division;
(d) a Count ordered by a court or arbitrator for a contest or proceeding, given under seal to that court or arbitrator only, and never to the Sender; and
(e) the Replies themselves, as delivered to the Sender under §5, but not their number.

(5) Neither the Register nor a Stream shall select or order the Sheets of a channel of the Class by reference to a Count.

§7 The class mark

(1) The class mark is the one-way arc: a point, and three arcs struck from that point on one side only, opening away from it, in the proportions of Schedule A.

(2) The Register keeps the class mark. Only a certified channel may use it, and only on its card, its Sheets and their sending records, while its certification stands.

(3) It is misuse of the class mark to
(a) use it for a channel that is not certified, or whose certification is suspended or withdrawn;
(b) use it on matter that is not a Publication of the Class, or with a service that collects data concerning Receivers or shows a Count;
(c) alter it, including by reversing it, closing its arcs into rings, adding a fourth arc, or adding any arc that faces the point;
(d) combine it with a speaker tag, a witness point, a network mark or another mark of grade, or use it so as to suggest a grade; or
(e) use it in place of the cross-delay arc (ASP Art. 11), or use that arc in place of it.

(4) Where the class mark is misused, the Register may require the misuse to cease, publish notice of it, and suspend or withdraw certification under §9. Misuse that presents the class mark as a mark of grade may also be presence fraud (ASP Art. 31).

(5) A channel that may use the class mark may print on its card the class line and, after it, the Register's line, in the following forms, unaltered:

  • class line, en: STANDOFFISH · OWP.
  • Register's line, en: Delivered at the speed of light. Eventually.

§8 In-transit provisions

(1) A Carrier that carries traffic for vessels In Transit shall publish in-transit provisions stating
(a) its Link Windows with each vessel, by schedule (stating the time scale) and direction;
(b) how it holds Replies for Senders In Transit under §5(3)(b);
(c) how it carries safety-of-life traffic;
(d) the ephemeris by which it computes the DAS;
(e) the operational traffic that it carries for vessels; and
(f) its carrier liability limit per Sheet, which shall be not lower than the floor in Schedule B.

(2) The captain of a vessel from which a channel of the Class sends shall adopt in-transit provisions for the vessel, consistent with the Carrier's, stating which terminals aboard may send; that no Read-back is received aboard for any channel of the Class while the vessel is In Transit; and, where Replies are held aboard, how the sealed store is kept.

(3) Where the Carrier's provisions and the vessel's provisions differ, the Carrier's prevail as to carriage and the vessel's as to conduct aboard. A dispute aboard under either is decided by the master's court, whose decision is valid aboard only and recognised by no court before Arrival, and after Arrival only through post-arrival retroactive recognition (ASP Arts. 52 and 53).

(4) These Terms are subject to a Carrier's in-transit provisions only where §2(5)(a), §5(3)(b) or this section so provides.

§9 Suspension and withdrawal of the class

(1) The Register may suspend or withdraw a channel's certification where
(a) its Sender breaches §2, §4 or §6, or sends a Sheet without a sending record;
(b) the class mark is misused for the channel;
(c) its Sender of record so requests;
(d) no Carrier that has accepted these Terms carries the channel;
(e) its Sender of record is unanchored (ASP Art. 33); or
(f) its speech is a source's whose NPR, or the NPR's named-party joint liability cover, is no longer on file with the Carrier.

(2) The Register shall give the Sender of record written notice of the ground and 30 days from receipt of the notice to answer.

(3) Where the Sender is In Transit, the Register may suspend certification for a breach of §2 or for misuse of the class mark, but shall not withdraw certification before Arrival; the period to answer runs from Arrival.

(4) On suspension or withdrawal the channel shall cease to use the class mark, and the Register shall mark the card accordingly. Sheets sent before then remain Publications of the Class, their sending records stand, and §6 continues to apply to them.

§10 Interpretation, precedence and conflicts

(1) These Terms may be cited as the One-Way Publication Terms (2066), the OWP Terms or OWP; a section may be cited as "OWP §2" and a subsection as "OWP §2(2)" or, within brackets, as "One-Way Publication Terms, 2066, §2". A reference to the STANDOFFISH terms is a reference to these Terms. STANDOFFISH is the Class's common name; One-Way Publication (OWP) is its legal name.

(2) These Terms are subject to the Three-Witness Convention (2047) (3WC), the Anchored Speech Protocol (ASP) and the Machine Clause (2073) (MC). Where these Terms conflict with any of those instruments, that instrument prevails.

(3) As to reception in any place, the law of that place prevails over these Terms. The jurisdiction notices on each sending record state the places provided for.

(4) Where these Terms conflict with a Carrier's in-transit provisions, these Terms prevail, except as §8(4) provides.

(5) The texts of these Terms in the Register's languages are equally authentic. Each is drafted in the form of its own language; they correspond section by section and subsection by subsection. Where the texts differ in meaning, the meaning that best reconciles them, having regard to the object of these Terms, is adopted. The legends (§4(3)), the class line and the Register's line (§7(5)) have a fixed form in each language; codes and notation are the same in all.

(6) Precedence: OWP Terms ≻ carrier file ≻ card. Where a card differs from the carrier file or from these Terms, these Terms and then the carrier file prevail.

(7) The Register may amend these Terms on 90 days' notice to every Sender of record and Carrier. An amendment does not apply to a Sheet sent before the amendment takes effect, and binds a Sender In Transit only from Arrival.

(8) Headings, the list of Schedules and the amendment history are not part of these Terms.
