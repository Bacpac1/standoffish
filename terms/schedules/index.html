Schedule A · The class mark: figure and proportions

OWP §7(1) · Amendment 3 (2079); r2091

(1) The class mark is a point and three arcs struck from the centre of the point, each through 180°, opening away from it on its right, as the drawing kept at the seat shows.

(2) The radius of the point is the unit. The centre lines of the arcs have radii of 1.96, 3.72 and 5.00, and their strokes are 0.42, 0.35 and 0.30 wide, from the inner arc out. Each stroke ends square on the vertical through the point.

(3) The figure stands in a box 6.15 wide and 10.30 high.

(4) A clear space of 5.00 surrounds the box on every side, clear of text, rules and other marks.

(5) The mark is printed in one colour, on a ground that gives it a contrast ratio of 4.5 to 1 or more.

(6) Across the outer arc, the mark measures 3 millimetres or more in print, and 12 pixels or more on a screen.

(7) The mark is reproduced whole and as drawn: at any size from the least, in its proportions, with its arcs on the right of the point.

(8) The drawing kept at the seat governs every copy.

Schedule B · Carrier liability limit: floor per Sheet

OWP §8(1)(f) · Amendment 4 (2086); r2091

(1) The carrier liability limit per Sheet that a Carrier sets in its in-transit provisions is 10 wage-hours or more.

(2) A wage-hour is valued at the median hourly wage of the place where the Carrier first received the Sheet, as last published there before the day of receipt: Earth's, for a Relay on Earth or within the light second of it; Mars's, for a Relay at Mars.

(3) The limits in force: the Chadu relays (CDX), 12 wage-hours; the Deimos relays (DMR), 10 wage-hours (their in-transit provisions, edition 2095.1). A Carrier may set a higher limit.

(4) A Sheet carried by two Carriers takes the limit of its Carrier of record, the Carrier whose Relay first received it. Claims go to the Carrier of record, which recovers from the performing Carrier under their agreement.

(5) A claim is made in writing to the Carrier of record, with the Sheet ID and the custody references of its sending record or of the Carrier's routing metadata. The Carrier answers in writing within 60 days.

(6) A claim may be made within two years of the day the Carrier enters the loss in its routing metadata, or, for a Sender In Transit, within two years of Arrival, whichever is later.

(7) The Carrier keeps the custody record of a lost Sheet with its carrier file until the time for claims has run, and of a Sheet under claim until the claim is settled.

(8) The Register reviews the floor with the Carriers of the class every five years; the next review falls in 2096.

Schedule C · Reply desks: hours and lodging numbers

OWP §5(1)–(3) · Amendment 4 (2086); r2091

(1) The Register keeps reply desks at the ground stations of the Chadu relays at Santa Maria (Azores), Malindi, Singapore, Wenchang, Honolulu and Kourou; at Relays CDX1 and CDX3, by link; and at Bocha Station, Deimos, for DMR1. Relays DMR2, DMR3 and DMR4 take Replies by link.

(2) A desk at a ground station keeps the station's hours, published on Lintel. A desk on a Relay takes Replies by link in the Relay's Link Windows. The desk at Bocha keeps the station's hours, in TCB.

(3) A desk gives a lodging number when it has received a Reply complete and entered it in its log.

(4) A lodging number is the desk's code, the ordinal date of lodging and the serial of the lodging on that day at that desk, as SIN 2095-302/0147. The desks' codes are SMA (Santa Maria), MYD (Malindi), SIN (Singapore), HAK (Wenchang), HNL (Honolulu), CAY (Kourou), CDX1, CDX3, BCH (Bocha), DMR2, DMR3 and DMR4. The date is in UTC on Earth and within the light second of it, and in TCB beyond (OWP §1(3)(a)).

(5) The lodging number records the lodging and no later event (OWP §5(2)).

(6) A desk forwards Replies in the order lodged, in the next Link Window for the Sheet's place of sending. A Reply to a Sheet of another Carrier goes on to that Carrier with its lodging number, and the Sheet keeps its Carrier of record.

(7) The Register lists the desks and their hours in its directory of desks, and enters a change there before it takes effect.

Schedule D · Channel codes: assignment and reservation

OWP §§1(3)(c), 3(1) · Amendment 4 (2086); r2091

(1) A channel code is three or four capital letters, A to Z.

(2) On certification the Register assigns a free code: the code the Sender asks for, where it is free, and otherwise the first free code, in the Register's order, formed from the letters of the channel's source tag or name. A three-letter code is assigned while one is free, and a four-letter code after.

(3) A code stays with its channel while the channel is in the class, through changes of particulars, suspension and review.

(4) When a channel leaves the class, its code stays with its Sheets and records. A three-letter code returns to the free codes 25 years after the day its channel left the class. A four-letter code is assigned once.

(5) A record keeps the code it was made with. Where a code has been assigned more than once, the Register's register of channels names each channel by the code and the dates of its assignment, and each Sheet by its ID and its date of sending.

(6) The Register keeps back from assignment the codes then entered for vessels, stations, Carriers and Relays in their registers; the codes and field names printed on cards and records, among them OWP, DAS, SRC, VPR, NPR, LND, MOI, TCB, UTC, EFF, LEAF and NULL; a code asked for in an application, for 90 days from the application; and a code that the Register finds would mislead.

(7) A Sheet ID is the channel code followed by the Sheet's serial: two digits from 01, and three after 99.

(8) The Register shows each code on the channel's card, and keeps the register of channels, with every assignment and its dates, at the seat.
