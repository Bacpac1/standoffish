§1 (extract). Definitions

(1) In this Clause:

"carrier" means a person who carries speech or images for others by any means of transmission;

"frame" means each still image of which a moving image is made, and "image" means a still or moving picture in any medium, including each frame of a moving image;

"label-to-name drift review" (LND review) means a review under §5A, and "LND threshold" (the label-to-name drift (LND) review threshold) means the period of continuous use set in Schedule 5;

"legal name" means a name that is entered in a register and that the law recognises as a name;

"machine" means a device or system that computes, and any model run on such a device or system, whatever its capability; a vessel, vehicle, station or building is not a machine by reason only that machines are installed in it;

"machine-made", of speech, text or an image, means made in whole or in part by a machine otherwise than by capture; an image captured from the light of a scene and corrected no further than Schedule 1 permits is not machine-made;

"named party of record" (NPR) has the meaning in §6(1), and "named-party joint liability cover" the meaning in §6(8);

"NULL", written as the value of a field in a record, means that the field can have no value in law; for a machine, the fields legal name, witness and signature are NULL (§2);

"phantasm" means a machine-made image, "phantasm stamp" (PS) means the stamp prescribed in §3, and "phantasm-stamp integrity check" means the check in §3(9);

"registry" means the register of machines kept under §5, and "registry number" (MR number) means the number assigned and written under §5(2);

"source" means a machine that produces speech; "source class" means the class entered for a source under §5(3), and is written in a record under the field name SRC; and "source tag" means a tag of a source;

"system-assigned tag" (system tag) means a tag that a system assigns to a source under §5(4);

"tag" means a designation of a machine, being its registry number or a system-assigned tag;

"terminal-resident", of a source, means of source class M2R;

"VPR" (voiceprint match ratio) means the score, from 0.00 to 1.00, by which a voiceprint check compares a voice with an enrolled voiceprint; a voice matches an enrolled voiceprint where VPR ≥ 0.80.

(2) This Clause may be cited as the Machine Clause (2073) or as MC, and a section as "MC §6".

§3. The phantasm stamp; the phantasm-stamp integrity check

(1) Every phantasm shall carry the phantasm stamp. A moving image shall carry it on every frame.

(2) The phantasm stamp stands in the top left corner of the image as shown (position TL), inset from the top and left edges as Schedule 1 provides. Where the image is shown within text that runs from right to left, the phantasm stamp may stand in the top right corner instead (position TR).

(3) The phantasm stamp is the figure in Schedule 1: a square, a square window concentric within it, and a solid square at its centre. Its side shall be ≥ 1/50 of the shorter side of the image as shown, and its contrast with the ground around it shall be not less than Schedule 1 requires. It is printed in a single ink or light; nothing shall be printed over it, and the picture may show through its window.

(4) The machine that makes a phantasm shall apply the phantasm stamp when the phantasm is made. No person shall show or publish, and no carrier shall carry, a phantasm that does not carry the phantasm stamp.

(5) An image any part of which is machine-made is a phantasm.

(6) A copy, excerpt, crop, still, print or re-encoding of a phantasm is a phantasm, and shall carry the phantasm stamp as this section requires.

(7) The phantasm stamp is not required on
(a) a collector record made and kept under ASP Art. 5, which carries the marks that Article prescribes; or
(b) a working view shown, in the course of making, only to the person operating the machine, and not published.

(8) A phantasm shall carry no mark of grade (ASP Art. 12). Removing, obscuring or occluding the phantasm stamp is presence fraud (ASP Art. 31(1)(f)).

(9) Any receiver may verify a phantasm stamp by the phantasm-stamp integrity check in Schedule 1, Part 2, which establishes whether the stamp shown is the stamp applied at making. A stamp that fails the check is obscured or occluded within ASP Art. 31(2).

§5. The registry

(1) The registry is kept under one numbering by the registrars whom the states party to the Convention appoint. Every machine that makes speech or images for others shall be entered in the registry before it is so used.

(2) The registry assigns each machine a registry number of 12 digits, which is never reassigned. The registry number is written "MR", a space, the 12 digits, a hyphen and a check digit computed from the 12 digits by the mod-10 method in Schedule 2 (MR dddddddddddd-c). In running text the 12 digits may be written in groups of three (ddd ddd ddd ddd), without the check digit.

(3) Every source shall be entered with its source class, written M n X, in which
(a) n is the host: 1 fixed installation; 2 personal terminal; 3 vessel or system; 4 autonomous body; and
(b) X is the residence: R resident, running on its host and on no other; T transient, moving between hosts; S service, running elsewhere and serving its host by link.

(4) Where speech is produced aboard a vessel or at a station with no person present at the collectors, and the voice matches no enrolled voiceprint (VPR below 0.80 at each of three checks), the system of the vessel or station shall treat the speech as a source's, mark it with the source mark (§4), and may assign the source a system-assigned tag. The system shall report the tag, the time of its assignment and the VPR values to the registry, for entry against the source's registry number.

(5) A system-assigned tag is a tag and not a legal name. No person shall enter a tag in any register as a legal name.

(6) Every machine shall bear a registry plate in the form of Schedule 4 showing its registry number and, for a source, the source mark. The legal-name field of the registry plate is NULL and shall be left empty.

§5A. Label-to-name drift review (inserted 2080; subsection (5) added 2091)

(1) Where a tag has been used continuously as the designation of a source, in publication or in any register, for longer than the LND threshold, an LND review of the tag shall be opened.

(2) The review is held by the body that keeps the register or publication in which the tag is used in public: for the source of a channel certified under the One-Way Publication Terms, the STANDOFFISH Register; otherwise the registrar that keeps the source's entry. A body other than that registrar that holds a review shall notify the registrar.

(3) On the evidence of the tag's use, the body holding the review shall decide that
(a) the tag stands, with a finding that it is not a legal name;
(b) the system that assigned the tag shall re-tag the source, and the tag is retired;
(c) the tag shall be used under a rotation schedule; or
(d) the tag's public use is suspended. Where the review raises the status of machines under §2, the body shall refer it to the Assembly under §12.

(4) Until the outcome of the review is published, the tag may be used; wherever it is shown, the pending review shall be shown with it; and the tag is not a legal name.

(5) For the source tag of a channel certified under the One-Way Publication Terms, the note that the Register enters on the channel's card (OWP §3(11)) shows the pending review wherever the channel is shown with its card.

§6. The named party of record; named-party joint liability cover

(1) Every machine shall have a named party of record (NPR): the person or institution entered in the registry as answering for the machine, who is its owner of record. A machine shall not be the NPR of a machine.

(2) The NPR is entered on registration and on every transfer. A transfer has effect when it is entered; until then, the person last entered remains the NPR.

(3) The NPR shall be disclosed
(a) to the registry, at all times;
(b) to every carrier that carries the machine's speech or phantasms, before the first carriage; and
(c) to a court, an arbitrator or a network, on its order in a contest or proceeding.

(4) A carrier to which an NPR is disclosed shall keep the NPR on file while it carries the machine's speech or phantasms and for 1 year after the last carriage, and shall disclose the NPR only to the registry or as subsection (3)(c) provides.

(5) A publication of a machine's speech or phantasms need not identify the NPR if the NPR is on file with every carrier that carries the publication; the publication shall then state that the NPR (owner of record) is on file with the carrier.

(6) The NPR answers for the machine's acts and omissions under this Clause and under ASP as if they were the NPR's own, unless the NPR proves that another person controlled the machine without the NPR's consent, in which case that other person answers. A machine answers for nothing in its own right.

(7) No carrier shall carry the speech or phantasms of a machine whose NPR is not on file with it.

(8) Every NPR shall carry named-party joint liability cover, in not less than the amount set in Schedule 6 for the machine's source class or kind, under which the insurer answers jointly with the NPR for the machine's acts and omissions. Evidence of the cover shall be kept on file, with the NPR, by every carrier that carries the machine's speech or phantasms.
