Article 2 (extract). Definitions

In this Protocol:

"3WC" means the Three-Witness Convention (2047), to which this Protocol is annexed and of which it forms part; "ASP" means this Protocol, and an Article of this Protocol may be cited as "ASP Art. 9";

"anchoring" means the procedure held under Article 7;

"anchored person" means a person whose identity is A-grade and who is not unanchored (Article 33);

"annual LEAF allowance" (LEAF) means the 120 signature leaves allotted to a person for each calendar year under Article 15, zeroed at the end of the year under the year-end zeroing rule (Article 16); a signature leaf is spent when it is drawn from the LEAF at an anchoring under Article 18, including after LEAF pre-authorisation under Article 18A; and in a record "LEAF n" states that n signature leaves were drawn for the matter recorded, "LEAF 0" that none was drawn;

"collector" means a device that records a scene for an anchoring by one or more of the means in Schedule 1, and "collector record" means its record;

"collector-free zone" means a place designated under Article 46 in which no collector may operate;

"Delay at Sending" (DAS), of a statement sent from beyond the light second, means the light-time between the place of sending and the centre of the Earth at the moment of sending, as certified under Article 54;

"delay-adjusted contest deadline" means the time at which the period for contesting a statement made elsewhere and received on Earth ends:

delay-adjusted contest deadline = t(E,UTC) + 72 h + 2·DAS

where DAS = 0 for a statement sent from within the light second (Article 37); it may be written CW;

"Earth receipt", written t(E,UTC), means the time in UTC at which a statement sent from elsewhere is first delivered to a receiver on Earth, as certified under Article 54;

"Earth–Mars mutual recognition schedule" means Schedule 6, made under Article 51A, which lists the types of instrument that the courts on Earth and the Mars Local Courts recognise from each other; until the Assembly adds anchorings to it, it covers no anchoring;

"light-time" (LT) means the time that light takes to pass one way between two places at a stated moment; a light-second and a light-minute are 1 s and 1 min of light-time; and two places are within the light second of each other while the light-time between them is less than 1 s;

"mark of grade" means a mark listed in Article 11;

"passive-receipt exemption" means the exemption in Article 32 under which a person who receives matter in a collector-free zone does not commit private collection, provided that every collector that the person carries or controls there is switched off;

"post-arrival retroactive recognition" means the procedure under Article 53 by which the local courts of the place of arrival review the anchorings made, and the decisions of the master's court given, aboard a vessel in transit, and recognise those that meet this Protocol with effect from the time each was made;

"presence court" means a court constituted under Article 41, and "presence hearing" means a hearing of a presence court at which the parties and the witnesses attend in person;

"private collection" means operating a collector in a collector-free zone, or causing or permitting one to operate there (Article 32);

"speaker" means a person who makes a statement capable of being anchored;

"standing score" means the score that a standing bureau computes for a person from the entries in the standing record under Article 22 (signature leaves drawn, witnessing done, contests lost, findings of perjury); the receipt of matter of any grade is not entered in the standing record and enters no standing score; "tri-bureau standing score" means the three bureaus' standing scores for a person, and "tri-bureau median standing score" means their median, by which eligibility to witness is read (Article 23);

"statutory minimum retention for routing metadata" means the period set by Article 54(3), 400 days at the date of this text, for which a carrier licensed under Article 49 shall keep the times, relays and link windows of the traffic that it carries and the DAS that it certifies;

"witnessed", of a statement, means confirmed at its making by witnesses present under Article 7.

Article 9. The evidence grades; the grade code

(1) Every statement, identity and record has one of three evidence grades: A-grade (anchored), P-grade (present) or C-grade (clue). A C-grade statement, identity or record is a clue.

(2) A statement is A-grade if it is made at an anchoring held under Articles 4 to 8, at which the speaker spends a signature leaf (Article 18). An identity is A-grade when it is established at an anchoring; a record is A-grade when it is made at, and forms part of, an anchoring. An A-grade statement binds: it may be given in testimony, may make, vary or end a contract, and may be an oath.

(3) A statement is P-grade if the speaker makes it in person, in one space with the person who receives it (Article 4), and it is not anchored. Those present may rely on a P-grade statement at their own risk; it is not evidence and it binds no one.

(4) Every other statement, identity and record is C-grade, including any that is
(a) remote: received live from outside the space in which it was made;
(b) recorded: a recording, transcript or copy, including a recording, transcript or copy of an A-grade or P-grade statement; or
(c) machine-made within the meaning of the Machine Clause (2073).

(5) A clue may suggest; it does not prove. A clue is not evidence, and in a presence hearing or other proceeding it may be used only as a ground for inquiry (Article 10).

(6) A statement received across a light-time of 1 s or more is C-grade where it is received, whatever its grade where it was made, except as Article 50 provides.

(7) No statement gains a grade by being copied, transmitted, repeated, published or marked; a restatement loses grade under the restatement-chain step-down rule (Article 13). A clue becomes A-grade only when the speaker makes it anew, in person, at an anchoring, or when a repeater repeats it under Article 50; the A-grade statement is then the new statement.

(8) Clues are not contestable. A statement that a clue attributes to an anchored person may be contested under Articles 36 and 37.

(9) A record that states an evidence grade shall state it as a grade code of the form G O-D/J, in which
(a) G is the evidence grade: A, P or C;
(b) O is the origin: 1 made in person, in one space with the person who receives it; 2 recorded (paragraph (4)(b)); 3 remote (paragraph (4)(a)); 4 machine-made (paragraph (4)(c)); where more than one origin applies, the highest number is written;
(c) D is the delay class of the light-time between the place where the matter was made and the centre of the Earth, at the moment of sending or, for matter not sent, of making: W less than 10 s; L 10 s or more and less than 180 s; D 180 s or more; and
(d) J is the place where the matter was made: E on Earth or within the light second of Earth; M on Mars or on a moon of Mars; T aboard a vessel in transit; S at any other station. An A-grade or P-grade code has origin 1; a C-grade code has origin 2, 3 or 4. The grade and the origin may be written alone, as "C4".

Article 13. The restatement-chain step-down rule

(1) A restatement is a statement by which a person repeats, reports or summarises a statement of another.

(2) As evidence of the statement restated, each restatement in a chain of restatements has an evidence grade one step below that of the statement that it restates: an A-grade statement restated is at most P-grade; a P-grade statement restated is C-grade; and C-grade is the floor.

(3) A recording, transcript, copy or transmission is C-grade under Article 9(4) wherever it stands in the chain, and a restatement by a source is machine-made (Article 9(4)(c)).

Article 31. Presence fraud

(1) Each of the following acts, and causing or permitting another person to do it, is presence fraud:
(a) appearing at an anchoring in another person's place, or causing another person to appear in one's own place (a stand-in);
(b) making a false collector record; altering, suppressing or substituting a collector record; or presenting a record so made or altered as genuine;
(c) presenting machine-made material as a scene or as a recording of a scene;
(d) presenting a clue, or any part or frame of a clue, as witnessed, as A-grade or as P-grade;
(e) applying a mark of grade, a witness point or a network mark to anything that does not have the grade that the mark signifies, including any machine-made material; and
(f) removing, obscuring or occluding the phantasm stamp (MC §3) on machine-made material, or presenting such material knowing that its phantasm stamp has been removed, obscured or occluded.

(2) For paragraph (1)(f), a phantasm stamp is obscured or occluded if it is cropped, covered or overprinted; reduced below the size or contrast that MC §3(3) requires; altered so that it can no longer be read as the phantasm stamp; or found by the phantasm-stamp integrity check (MC §3(9)) not to be the stamp applied at making.

(3) A mark that an image shows as part of what the image depicts is not applied to that image for the purposes of paragraph (1)(e), provided that the image carries the phantasm stamp.

(4) It is a defence to a charge under paragraph (1)(c), (d) or (f) that the person charged did not know, and had no reason to suspect, that the material was machine-made, was a clue or had lost its phantasm stamp.

(5) Presence fraud is the gravest offence under this Protocol. A person found to have committed presence fraud shall be unanchored under Article 33, and the finding shall be entered in the standing record (Article 22).

(6) Where an act in paragraph (1) is done by a machine, the machine's named party of record (NPR) answers for the act under MC §6, and the machine shall be shut down under MC §7.

(7) A change of body is not presence fraud by reason only that it was not reported; it is dealt with under Articles 26 and 34.

Article 50. Agreements made beyond the light second: repetition in person on Earth; repeaters

(1) A statement anchored beyond the light second of Earth, including an agreement, is C-grade on Earth (Article 9(6)) and binds there only from the time at which it is repeated in person on Earth at an anchoring. The repeat is A-grade on Earth from that time.

(2) The repeat may be made by the speaker, or by an anchored person (a repeater) under a power to repeat that the speaker anchored on Earth before leaving the light second of Earth. The power names the repeater and states the matters, or the kinds of statement, that the repeater may repeat.

(3) A repeater repeats the statement as received, without addition or omission, and states at the anchoring the power under which the repeat is made. At each repeat the repeater draws one signature leaf from the repeater's own annual LEAF allowance (Article 18); a repeat draws nothing from the speaker's.

(4) A repeat under this Article is not a restatement within Article 13. The statement repeated binds the speaker on Earth as if the speaker had made it at the anchoring at which it is repeated.
