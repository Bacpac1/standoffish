The gazetteer gives the codes printed in coded lines, the short names and places printed on cards, and the places and rules that the jurisdiction notices on sending records name. Anchor networks are named by their registered names, CIVIC, CONSTANT, MUTUAL and OATH. The OWP Terms and a Carrier's in-transit provisions prevail over it.

The Moon's stations

  • Stations with rotating crews, 1.19 to 1.36 light-seconds from Earth: beyond the light second (ASP Art. 4). Each answers to the state that registers it, a party to 3WC; presence hearings sit on Earth, on the crew's return (ASP Art. 41).
  • The Earth notice applies to reception there, by the Register's direction of 1 November 2066.
  • An agreement made there takes effect on Earth when repeated in person (ASP Art. 50). An anchoring made there counts on Earth as a clue; crews anchor before departure or after their return.
  • Matter made there carries place letter S and delay class W; events there are stated in TCB (OWP §1(3)(a)).

Mars Local Courts · MLC

  • The local courts constituted at Mars under ASP Art. 51, beyond the light second. Their jurisdiction covers Mars and its moons, Bocha Station included, and vessels berthed there.
  • Their rulings bind at Mars and reach Earth as clues (ASP Arts. 9(6), 42).
  • Recognition between Earth and Mars is by the Earth–Mars mutual recognition schedule (ASP Art. 51A, Schedule 6), which covers no anchoring. Until it does, an anchoring made on Earth is a clue at Mars, and one made at Mars a clue on Earth.
  • For a vessel arriving at Mars, the MLC review the anchorings made and the master's court decisions given aboard in transit (post-arrival retroactive recognition, ASP Art. 53).

The master's court · the captain's temporary court

  • Held by the captain aboard a vessel In Transit (ASP Art. 52; OWP §1(1)), from unberthing until Arrival.
  • Decides disputes aboard by the master's court rules, including disputes under the Carrier's and the vessel's in-transit provisions (OWP §8(3)).
  • Its decisions, and the anchorings made aboard, are valid aboard only and recognised by no court before Arrival; after Arrival, only through post-arrival retroactive recognition (ASP Art. 53).

OATH collector-free zones

  • Collector-free zones are places designated under ASP Art. 46 in which no collector may operate. OATH, the network of religious and legal-tradition houses, takes oath anchorings in them, of people only.
  • Operating a collector in a collector-free zone is private collection (ASP Art. 32). Receiving there is not, provided every collector that the receiver carries or controls is switched off: the passive-receipt exemption (ASP Art. 32, 2072).
  • The notice names OATH zones, the commonest; the exemption applies in every collector-free zone.
