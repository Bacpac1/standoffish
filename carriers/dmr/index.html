Edition 2095.1, in force from 1 March 2095.

(a) Link Windows

Windows are scheduled in TCB and numbered LW ddd/nn, by the ordinal day of opening and the order of opening that day across all the relays' windows, about 20 a day (OWP §1(3)(b)).

Window With Each day Relay Carries
The trunk the Chadu relays, over the Earth–Mars trunk twelve, of 2 hours, opening at the even hours DMR1 to DMR4 in turn the traffic between Mars and Earth
A transit a local craft between Bocha Station, Stickney Station and the surface stations one for each transit, from unberthing to Arrival the relay in sight all the craft's traffic, Publications included
Approach and departure a transfer vessel from MOI to berthing at Bocha, and from unberthing at Bocha to its departure from Mars one, 16:00–20:00 DMR1 the vessel's operational traffic

A trunk window carries a number in each carrier's count, and a record prints the number in its Carrier of record's count. A transfer vessel's Publications go in the windows of its Carrier of record: for the five vessels of 2095, the Chadu relays (their provisions (a)).

(b) Replies for Senders in transit

Replies lodged for a Sender aboard a local craft wait at the Register's desk at Bocha Station and are released on the craft's Arrival, to the station where it berths (OWP §5(3)(b)). Replies for a Sender aboard a transfer vessel go to the vessel's Carrier of record in the order lodged, for its next window, as its provisions set out.

(c) Safety-of-life traffic

Carried first: at the head of every window, ahead of all other traffic, and at all hours on the relays' distress link, which all four relays keep open. A distress call takes over any window in progress. Dust-storm warnings for the surface stations and for craft in transit come next.

(d) Ephemeris

The DAS is computed from the Chadu relays' ephemeris, edition CE 2095.2, which the two carriers use for the trunk by agreement, with planetary positions updated each 1 January and 1 July, and each vessel's orbit from its navigation report of the day. The relays certify the DAS and the time of Earth receipt of the Sheets that they carry, on request; the certified values govern (OWP §§3(5), 5(5)(b)).

(e) Operational traffic

Navigation: approach, berthing and landing; tracking and orbit updates. The surface stations' operations: power, habitat, surface traffic. MUTUAL's anchoring records at Mars. The notices of the Mars Local Courts to the presence courts and to the master's courts of arriving vessels. Medical consultation with Earth. Machine-registry entries for sources at Mars (MC §5). Mail, under the relays' mail terms. Operational traffic travels after safety-of-life traffic.

(f) Liability

The carrier liability limit is 10 wage-hours per Sheet, at the median wage of the place of lodging: OWP Schedule B's floor.

Interline carriage

The relays carry the trunk with the Chadu relays under an interline agreement. A Sheet's Carrier of record is the carrier whose Relay first receives it: the Deimos relays for Sheets sent at Mars; the Chadu relays for Sheets sent on Earth, within the light second, at the Moon's stations and aboard the vessels for which they hold windows. The Carrier of record keeps the carrier file (OWP §3(7)) and answers for the Sheet up to its own limit; the other carrier carries it as performing carrier. Each handover over the trunk is entered in the custody record, with the Sheet ID, both carriers, the time and the window. Claims go to the Carrier of record.

Conjunction

The trunk stands down while the Sun–Earth–Mars angle, computed from CE 2095.2, is under 2°. For the conjunction of 2096:

  • from 2096-202 (20 July), the trunk windows carry safety-of-life and operational traffic first;
  • from 2096-206 to 2096-216 (24 July to 3 August), the trunk stands down, and safety-of-life traffic goes on any link that holds;
  • from 2096-217 to 2096-220 (4 to 7 August), the trunk reopens window by window, after link checks;
  • from 2096-221 (8 August), the traffic held goes, oldest first, after safety-of-life traffic.

Traffic for Earth waits at DMR1, and traffic for Mars at the Chadu relays, in the order lodged. Windows at Mars run as scheduled, and the Register's desk at Bocha keeps its hours. The relays publish the windows affected 90 days ahead, with the Register and with MUTUAL.

Routing metadata

Kept for the statutory minimum retention, 400 days from sending, then erased (OWP §2(2); ASP Art. 54(3)).

The relays

Four relays: DMR1 at Bocha Station on Deimos, 23,460 km from the centre of Mars; DMR2, DMR3 and DMR4 in areostationary orbit, 17,032 km above the equator, over 60° E, 180° E and 300° E. The surface stations and Stickney Station hold a relay at all hours. The Register's desk at Bocha Station is the reply desk at DMR1; DMR2 to DMR4 take replies by link, and a reply lodged at one is lodged at all (OWP §5(1)).
